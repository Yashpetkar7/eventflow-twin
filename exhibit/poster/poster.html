The challenge

GITEX Global 2025 was expected to bring 200,000+ visitors to Dubai World Trade Centre over five days. Events like this squeeze thousands of metro trips into a few hours at a few stations. Planners need to know where capacity breaks – before it happens – and which response works best.

How it works

RTA timetable (GTFS) + OpenStreetMap→ Forecast: weekday profile + event surge→ Hotspots over 85 %→ Planning agent proposes→ Simulator measures every option→ Human approves→ Before / after + log

The AI chooses and explains; it never calculates. Every number on screen comes from the simulator and is checked.

What's new

  • Event-aware what-if planning for the hours around a big event, station by station
  • Multimodal responses compared side by side: extra trains, wayfinding, feeder shuttles, robotaxi staging, staggered exits
  • Auditable AI: numbers only from the simulator, automatic number check, nothing applied without a human

Built around RTA AI Strategy priorities (alignment, not endorsement)

1

Congestion reduction – measures the over-capacity station-hours each plan removes

2

Agentic AI – a planning agent proposes and compares complete plans; the simulator produces every number

3

Human approval – nothing is applied without a planner's OK, and every decision is logged

Data & limits

RTA open data (official GTFS metro timetable) · map © OpenStreetMap contributors. Station demand is synthetic and labelled until RTA's metro ridership (now open on data.dubai) is ingested. Attendance, metro share and train fullness are assumptions. Research prototype – not an operational RTA system.
